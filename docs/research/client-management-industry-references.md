# Client/contact data and invoice-recipient references

**Research accessed:** 2026-11-17  
**Scope:** Official help and API references for Zoho Books, QuickBooks Online (QBO), and Xero, focused on customer/contact records and invoice workflows. Features, names, plan eligibility, and behavior can vary by region, subscription, and product version; the cited QuickBooks support pages are primarily US-edition pages, Zoho Help routed to its India English edition for some pages, and Xero publishes regional help/API details. [Z1][Z3][Q3][Q5][Q8][X1][X2][X3]

This is a research note, not a product specification. Product behavior below is sourced; recommendations and inferences are separated at the end.

## Current app boundary (repository facts)

- The app resolves an authenticated `User` to an `Organization` through `OrganizationMembership`. The accepted ADR says missing or ambiguous membership fails closed, and that each `Client` row belongs to an `Organization`; it does not define customer-facing Client authentication. See [`docs/adr/0002-organization-access.md`](../adr/0002-organization-access.md) and [`app/middleware/organization-context.ts`](../../app/middleware/organization-context.ts).
- The current `clients` table has an individual/business type, legal/preferred/trading names, one top-level email/phone, one named contact with role/email/phone, a billing address, currency, tax fields, and active/archived state. It does not currently have a contact-person collection, a shipping/additional address collection, client notes, or client-level payment-term defaults. See [`app/data/db.ts`](../../app/data/db.ts).
- The invoice table is Organization-scoped, links to a Client, and stores recipient name, billing-address fields, tax details, currency, and payment terms on the invoice. The schema comment says draft recipient details are copied from the Client when the draft is created. See [`app/data/db.ts`](../../app/data/db.ts) and [`app/data/invoices/records.ts`](../../app/data/invoices/records.ts).

## Sourced product findings

### Zoho Books

**Record shape and defaults.** Zoho Books lets a customer be explicitly typed as **Business** or **Individual**. Its creation form documents a primary contact, company name, display name, email, phone, tax treatment/identifiers, preferred currency, payment terms, credit limit, billing and shipping addresses, contact persons, custom fields, reporting tags, and internal remarks. Different-currency customer settings are plan-dependent. [Z1]

**Contact people and addresses.** A customer may have no contact person or as many as 500 associated contact persons. A contact person can be used for communication and portal-related activities. Billing and shipping are the two default addresses; additional addresses can be added and selected for transactions. [Z2]

**Tax, currency, and notes.** The Contacts API describes region-dependent tax fields rather than one globally uniform identifier: examples include US `tax_id_type`/`tax_id_value`, UK/Avalara `vat_reg_no`, and GCC/Mexico/Kenya/South Africa `tax_reg_no`. It also documents currency, payment terms, credit limit, notes, custom fields, and address objects. [Z4] Zoho’s per-contact multi-currency transaction mode is plan-dependent and must be enabled; after transactions exist in other currencies, the preference cannot be disabled. [Z3] Customer creation calls remarks internal notes/comments, and the customer-comments help says those comments are not shown to customers; invoice `notes`, by contrast, are described as visible on the invoice document. [Z1][Z2][Z5][Z6]

**Duplicate handling and lifecycle.** Zoho has an explicit preference to allow duplicate customer display names; customer imports also offer skip, overwrite, or add-as-new handling. A merge action is documented for duplicate customers. [Z1][Z3][Z2] If a customer is temporarily not being used, Zoho recommends marking it inactive. Permanent deletion requires deleting all sales/purchase transactions and associated journal entries first. [Z2]

**Invoice link and recipient details.** Invoice creation requires a `customer_id` belonging to the specified organization, and can select contact persons for invoice communications. The invoice API also exposes invoice-level billing/shipping address data, saved address IDs, an optional one-time shipping address, and endpoints to update invoice addresses. This supports transaction-specific address data, but the API does not promise that every recipient field is immutable after invoice creation. [Z5]

**Customer-facing portal.** Zoho Books documents a dedicated Customer Portal. The organization invites selected customer contact persons by email; each person signs up with a password. Depending on the organization’s settings, customers can view transactions and statements, accept quotes, pay invoices, track payments, comment, and edit their own contact details. Profile edits made in the portal update the corresponding customer record. Portal preferences can restrict some customer actions. [Z7][Z8]

### QuickBooks Online

**Record shape and defaults.** The QBO Customer API represents a person or organization through `DisplayName`, person-name fields, and `CompanyName`; it also documents a customer-type reference, parent/sub-customer hierarchy, primary email and phone fields, billing/shipping addresses, taxable status, sales terms, a payment-method reference, and preferred delivery method. The add-customer help describes a required display name, optional parent/sub-customer relationship, tax-exemption settings, and internal notes. [Q1][Q2]

**Contact channels and addresses.** QBO’s February 2026 release notes say that multiple email addresses can now be stored on one customer profile and used to send communications to different contacts at the same company. That is profile-level multi-email support; the Customer API fields cited above do not describe a Zoho/Xero-style structured list of named contact persons. [Q1][Q3] QBO also supports up to 30 shipping addresses for one customer; the cited help documents those as multiple shipping locations, separate from the billing address. [Q4]

**Tax, currency, payment defaults, and notes.** The Customer API documents `Taxable`, a default tax-code reference, a resale number, and a secondary tax identifier whose meaning varies by locale (for example, UK UTR or India CST registration number). US help describes customer taxability and exemption reasons; this is not evidence of a single global tax-ID format. [Q1][Q7] When multicurrency is enabled, QBO can assign a currency to a customer; once transactions have been recorded, the profile currency cannot be changed. The cited help lists Essentials, Plus, and Advanced for assigning a foreign currency, with differences for Intuit Enterprise Suite. [Q5] The Customer API exposes sales terms, payment method, and delivery preference. [Q1] Enhanced customer-profile custom fields require QBO Advanced and are limited to 12 active fields per profile type; supported types include text/number, date, and dropdown. [Q8] Intuit’s add-customer help calls customer notes “internal” and says they appear on invoices. The Invoice API separately distinguishes an organization-private `PrivateNote` from a customer-visible `CustomerMemo`; keep those concepts distinct. [Q2][Q11]

**Duplicate handling and lifecycle.** QBO’s API requires `DisplayName` to be unique across customer, employee, and vendor records. Its merge flow requires matching display names, is permanent/irreversible, makes the duplicate inactive, and moves the duplicate’s transactions to the retained profile. Deleting a customer with transactions is implemented as making the customer inactive; the transaction history remains available in reports, and the customer can be reactivated. [Q1][Q10][Q9]

**Invoice link and historical recipient details.** An invoice requires a `CustomerRef` and can carry `BillEmail`, `BillAddr`, and `ShipAddr`; if invoice addresses are omitted, QBO fills them from the referenced customer. [Q11] Intuit’s customer-edit help says changing customer details updates previously sent invoices or recurring transactions that use those details, including billing/shipping/email addresses, tax status, payment methods, and terms. [Q6]

**Customer-facing access.** QBO help documents emailed invoice links and a Checkout Portal where customers can select/pay multiple sent invoices that are currently due. A separate secure portal link lets a customer manage a saved payment method or remove the seller’s access to it; that portal is specifically about the payment method, not general profile or transaction-history management. [Q12][Q13] An older Intuit-hosted Community response said QBO did not offer a general portal for customer transaction history. Because it predates the 2026 Checkout Portal flow, treat it as dated support guidance rather than a definitive current product guarantee. [Q14] The QuickBooks Business Network is another distinct feature: businesses connect by invitation and exchange/sync business contact information; the cited help does not describe it as a customer login to a seller’s account. [Q15]

### Xero

**Record shape and contact people.** Xero’s Accounting API describes a contact `Name` as the full name of a contact or organization, with optional first/last names, company number, email, phones, tax number/type, addresses, default currency, and customer/supplier status. Customer or supplier status is reflected by transactions entered for that contact. A contact can have up to five additional `ContactPersons`; the help requires the primary person to have an email before adding additional people. [X5][X16][X1] The primary person is not automatically inserted into transactions; the billing-address “Attention” field can direct invoices/statements to a person. Additional people can be included in invoice reminders. [X1][X18]

**Addresses, tax, currency, and defaults.** Xero Accounting contacts have one billing and one delivery address. The billing address is used on transactions, including paid items; editing an address from a transaction updates the contact record. [X4][X1] Tax identifiers are localized: the API identifies `TaxNumber` as an ABN, GST number, VAT number, or Tax ID depending on the regionalized version, with a regional `TaxNumberType`; US help describes Tax ID/W-9 details and UK help documents VAT details. [X5][X2][X3] If the plan includes multicurrency, a contact can have a default currency that applies to new transactions and can be overridden per transaction. Xero also supports contact-level default due dates, sales/purchase accounts, tax rates, sales discount, credit limit, and invoice branding theme. [X4][X1][X8]

**Notes and custom fields.** The standard Accounting contact fields include contact notes and an account number for a registration, membership, or other reference. [X1] The custom-field documentation located is for Xero Practice Manager, a separate practice-management product; it should not be assumed to describe custom fields on the standard Xero Accounting contact record. [X12][X5]

**Duplicates and lifecycle.** Xero’s contact help says contact names must be unique, while the Developer API warns that this rule may change and recommends `ContactID` as the stable identifier. [X1][X5] Xero’s merge flow archives the merged contact and keeps related invoices/bills; the merge article says invoices inherit the kept contact’s name. [X9] Separately, Xero’s transaction-search help says transactions for a renamed or merged contact can display the original name, so the documented historical-name presentation is not fully consistent across help pages. [X17] Standard contacts can be archived but not deleted; archived contacts remain on reports and existing transactions, cannot be used for new transactions, and can be restored individually. [X10][X7] Xero documents a separate support-assisted “deep archive” for verified personal-data erasure requests; transactions remain viewable even though the contact itself cannot be searched, viewed, or edited. [X11]

**Invoice link and historical recipient details.** Xero’s invoice API requires the invoice to refer to a `ContactID`; it says the default invoice address comes from the contact’s postal address and cannot generally be manually overridden per invoice (US automated-sales-tax address fields are a separate case). [X6] Contact help says name changes do not affect existing invoices, while Xero’s data-rectification help says updating a contact can update most of its transactions, including invoices and purchase orders. The contact-address help also says billing address appears on transactions and can be updated from one. [X1][X11][X4]

**Customer-facing access.** Xero supports online invoice links and configured “Pay Now” payment services; customers do not need a Xero login just to pay an online invoice. [X15] Separately, an invoice contact can create a free Xero login from an invoice’s **Outstanding bills** link and see outstanding invoices/credits and a real-time statement for that relationship. [X13] The product named **Xero portal** is a separate secure destination for documents sent by an accountant or bookkeeper, such as reports or tax returns. [X14]

**Product-boundary note.** Xero Practice Manager separately defines practice-side clients and contacts; its help says one contact can be associated with multiple practice clients. This is a Practice Manager relationship, not the standard Xero Accounting contact/API model or the customer-facing Xero portal. [X19]

## Recommendations and inferences (not product facts)

1. **Keep the seller access boundary separate from the customer record.** The app’s `OrganizationMembership` authorizes an internal `User` to work in an Organization; the product examples show that customer portal access, where available, is a separate invitation/authentication flow scoped to customer-facing transactions. If a portal is ever specified, design a separate customer identity and explicit organization/client authorization instead of granting a customer internal `OrganizationMembership`. This is an architectural inference from the app ADR and sourced portal patterns, not a claim that the app currently has a portal. See [`docs/adr/0002-organization-access.md`](../adr/0002-organization-access.md). [Z7][X13][X14]
2. **Do not assume a globally shared Client across Organizations.** Zoho’s invoice API requires a customer ID in the same organization; Xero’s API says `ContactID` is unique within an organization. Those sources do not establish a universal cross-organization customer identity. Keep Clients Organization-scoped unless a product decision explicitly requires shared contacts and defines their ownership/privacy rules. See [`docs/adr/0002-organization-access.md`](../adr/0002-organization-access.md). [Z5][X5]
3. **Separate the account from the people and their channels.** The app distinguishes individual/business and has legal, preferred, and trading name fields, but stores one named contact. A future enhancement could model multiple named contact persons with role/department, email/phone, and explicit invoice-recipient selection. A list of emails alone should not be treated as a structured person directory. [Inference based on Z2, Q3, X1]
4. **Treat master data as defaults and invoices as historical documents.** Keep client-level currency, payment terms, tax treatment, and preferred payment method as defaults, while allowing transaction-level overrides where appropriate. Snapshot recipient name, contact person/email, billing/shipping addresses, and relevant tax identifiers onto an invoice at issue/send time; preserve issued values when a Client is edited, and use an explicit correction workflow for issued documents. This is recommended because the products’ historical-edit behavior differs. [Inference based on Z1, Z5, Q1, Q6, Q11, X1, X6, X11]
5. **Use address roles and jurisdiction-tagged tax identifiers.** The current Client has a billing address and generic tax fields; consider separate delivery/shipping addresses and additional saved addresses only when needed. Model tax identifiers with an issuing jurisdiction/type and avoid applying one country-independent validation rule. Keep public invoice notes separate from internal Client notes/comments. [Inference based on app schema, Z1–Z6, Q1–Q2, X1–X3]
6. **Prefer reversible archive and deliberate merge over hard deletion.** Keep invoice-to-client references intact; make archive reversible; require review/confirmation when merging and define how invoice recipient snapshots, contact-person links, and notes are handled. Product merge workflows vary and can be irreversible. [Inference based on Z2, Q9–Q10, X9–X10]

## Cross-product synthesis (sourced facts)

- All three invoice schemas link an invoice to a customer/contact reference. Zoho explicitly requires the customer ID to belong to the same organization as the invoice, and Xero says `ContactID` is unique within an organization. Their identity and person/address models differ: Zoho has an explicit Individual/Business type and up to 500 contact persons; Xero has a unified accounting contact with a primary person and up to five additional people; QBO documents profile-level multiple email addresses and up to 30 shipping locations. [Z1][Z2][Z5][Q1][Q3][Q4][Q11][X1][X5][X6]
- Currency, terms, payment, custom-field, and tax defaults are product- and plan-specific; tax identifiers are localized rather than one portable global field. [Z1][Z3][Z4][Q1][Q5][Q8][X1][X2][X3]
- Lifecycle and invoice history are not interchangeable: QBO makes deletion inactive and explicitly updates some previously sent invoices after customer edits; Zoho requires linked transactions to be deleted before permanent customer deletion and exposes editable invoice address fields; Xero archives contacts and documents both preserved and mutable historical details depending on field/workflow. [Z2][Q6][Q9][Q10][Q11][X1][X4][X6][X9][X10][X11][X17]
- Customer access ranges from Zoho’s transaction portal, to QBO’s invoice/payment and saved-method flows, to Xero’s online invoice and limited outstanding-bills login; Xero’s named portal is for accountant/bookkeeper documents. [Z7][Q12][Q13][X13][X14][X15]

**App boundary:** the accepted ADR resolves seller-side `User` access to an `Organization` through `OrganizationMembership` and scopes `Client` records to that Organization. A customer login/portal would be a separate app feature and authorization path, not an implication of the existing membership model. [App-specific fact: `docs/adr/0002-organization-access.md`](../adr/0002-organization-access.md); portal distinction is an inference from the sourced product workflows above.
